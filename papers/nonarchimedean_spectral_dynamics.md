## Abstract

We establish a rigorous mathematical theory of discrete non-Archimedean transfer operators, character-space orbit factorizations, and dynamical trace formulas over finite abelian rings, and connect these algebraic structures to provably coherent geometric representations in sequential attention systems. On the cyclic ring $\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$, we prove that the directed relation operator $D_n$ generated by the dyadic affine branchings $x \mapsto 3x$ and $x \mapsto 3x - 1$ acts as a monomial permutation matrix in the Pontryagin character basis $\chi_k(x) = \omega^{kx}$. We show that the multiplicative action of $3 \in (\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ on odd residues partitions the non-trivial characters into two conjugate cyclic orbits of length $2^{n-2}$, evaluating the cyclotomic orbit product to $\pm i \sqrt{2}$ and proving the matrix power theorem $S_n^{2^{n-1}} = -2 \cdot \mathbf{I}$ on the twisted sector. This proves that all non-zero eigenvalues of $S_n$ lie on concentric spectral circles of radius $|\lambda| = 2^{2^{-(n-1)}}$, which condense onto the unit circle as $n \to \infty$ with critical scaling exponent $\sigma = 1/2$. 

On the affine group $\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})$, we derive the discrete Schreier dynamical fixed-point trace formula for all operator powers $D_n^m$, establishing an exact vanishing theorem for all intermediate powers $1 \le m < 2^{n-2}$ and evaluating the non-trivial orbit return trace at $m = 2^{n-2}$. We formulate the 2-adic stationary Markov chain as a direct limit of finite matrix algebras, proving that the stationary dimension group is the ordered group of dyadic rationals $(K_0(M_{2^\infty}), K_0^+, [1]) \cong (\mathbb{Z}[1/2], \mathbb{Z}[1/2]^+, 1)$, and prove the discrete Ryu-Takayanagi minimal cut entropy $S(A) = k \ln 2$ on finite binary tree tensor networks. Translating these non-Archimedean structures to sequential representations, we formalize the $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ rotational geometry of Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), proving that arithmetic key centroid averaging strictly attenuates key norms ($\|k_{\mathrm{avg}}\| < \|k_0\|$) and destroys rotational phase coherence, whereas medoid key anchor selection preserves relative angular phase shifts identically, with exact logit scaling under synthetic temporal variance decay. Finally, we establish the Multi-Prime DAG Treewidth Covering Theorem, proving that adèlic multi-prime tree routing across coprime bases $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, \dots, p_G\}$ provides additive routing capacity $\mathrm{Cap}(\mathcal{P}, N) = \sum_{g=1}^G \lfloor \log_{p_g} N \rfloor$ that covers causal dependency structures of bounded hierarchical width while guaranteeing exponential sparsity in tree depth. In parallel, modern deep neural sequence models and long-context Transformer architectures face fundamental scalability limitations arising from the quadratic complexity of dense self-attention and the linear memory growth of positional Key-Value (KV) caches. Non-Archimedean metrics and ultrametric tree structures provide a natural inductive prior for hierarchical sequence parsing and block-sparse attention routing. ## 2. Pontryagin Character Action and Monomial Transfer Operators

### 2.1 The Directed Relation Operator $D_n$

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$ denote the finite cyclic ring of order $N = 2^n$. We consider the directed relation matrix $D_n \in \mathrm{Mat}_{N \times N}(\mathbb{Q})$ whose entries encode the two affine transitions $x \mapsto 3x$ and $x \mapsto 3x - 1$:

$$
D_n(x, y) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } y \equiv 3x \pmod{2^n} \text{ or } y \equiv 3x - 1 \pmod{2^n}, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

For every $x \in \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$, the two branch targets $3x$ and $3x - 1$ are distinct modulo $2^n$ since $3x - (3x - 1) = 1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{2^n}$ for $n \ge 1$. Consequently, every row of $D_n$ contains exactly two non-zero entries, and $D_n$ defines a bounded linear operator on the space of complex-valued functions $\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}}$.

### 2.2 Pontryagin Characters and the Monomial Action

Let $\zeta = e^{2\pi i / 2^n}$ be a primitive $2^n$-th root of unity in $\mathbb{C}$. The Pontryagin dual group $(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\vee$ consists of the additive characters $\chi_k \colon \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ defined for $k \in \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$ by:

$$
\chi_k(x) = \zeta^{k x} = \exp\left(\frac{2\pi i k x}{2^n}\right)
$$

The collection $\{\chi_k\}_{k \in \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}}$ forms an orthogonal Fourier basis for $L^2(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})$.

```lean
    (1 + χ (-k)) * χ (3 * k * x)
```

#### Theorem 2.1 (Character-Space Monomial Action)
For every $n \ge 1$ and every character index $k \in \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$, the action of the adjoint operator $D_n^\top$ on the basis function $\chi_k$ is given by:

$$
(D_n \chi_k)(x) = \sum_{y \in \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}} D_n(x, y) \chi_k(y) = (1 + \chi_k(-1)) \chi_{3k}(x) = (1 + \zeta^{-k}) \chi_{3k}(x)
$$

*Proof.* Expanding the sum over the non-zero entries of row $x$:

$$
\sum_{y} D_n(x, y) \chi_k(y) = \chi_k(3x) + \chi_k(3x - 1) = \zeta^{k(3x)} + \zeta^{k(3x - 1)} = \zeta^{3kx} (1 + \zeta^{-k}) = (1 + \zeta^{-k}) \chi_{3k}(x)
$$

This completes the proof. $\square$

Thus, in the character basis, $D_n$ acts as a **monomial operator**: it permutes the basis elements according to the multiplicative map $k \mapsto 3k \pmod{2^n}$ while multiplying by the position-dependent weight factor $w(k) = 1 + \zeta^{-k}$.

### 2.3 General Monomial Endomorphisms and Matrix Powers

Let $X$ be a finite set, $\pi \colon X \to X$ a permutation, and $w \colon X \to R$ a weight function taking values in a commutative ring $R$. The associated monomial endomorphism $T_{\pi, w} \colon R^X \to R^X$ is defined by:

$$
(T_{\pi, w} f)(x) = w(x) \cdot f(\pi(x))
$$

In matrix form, the entry at $(i, j)$ is given by $(M_{\pi, w})_{i, j} = w(i) \delta_{j, \pi(i)}$.

#### Theorem 2.2 (Monomial Orbit Power Identity)
Let $k \in \mathbb{N}$. The $k$-th power of the monomial endomorphism $T_{\pi, w}$ acts on $f \in R^X$ by:

$$
(T_{\pi, w}^k f)(x) = \left( \prod_{j=0}^{k-1} w(\pi^j(x)) \right) f(\pi^k(x))
$$

Furthermore, if $\pi^{2L} = \mathrm{id}_X$ and the cumulative weight product along every orbit of length $2L$ satisfies:

$$
\prod_{j=0}^{2L-1} w(\pi^j(x)) = -c \quad (\forall x \in X)
$$

for some scalar $c \in R$, then the operator power satisfies:

$$
T_{\pi, w}^{2L} = -c \cdot \mathrm{id}_{R^X}, \qquad M_{\pi, w}^{2L} = -c \cdot \mathbf{I}_{|X|}
$$

*Proof.* The proof proceeds by induction on $k$. For $k = 0$, $T_{\pi, w}^0 = \mathrm{id}$ and the empty product is $1$. Assuming the identity holds for $k$, the composition gives:

$$
(T_{\pi, w}^{k+1} f)(x) = T_{\pi, w}(T_{\pi, w}^k f)(x) = w(x) (T_{\pi, w}^k f)(\pi(x)) = w(x) \left(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1} w(\pi^j(\pi(x)))\right) f(\pi^k(\pi(x))) = \left(\prod_{j=0}^k w(\pi^j(x))\right) f(\pi^{k+1}(x))
$$

Setting $k = 2L$, the assumption $\pi^{2L}(x) = x$ and the weight product identity yield $(T_{\pi, w}^{2L} f)(x) = -c \cdot f(x)$ for all $x$, proving $T_{\pi, w}^{2L} = -c \cdot \mathrm{id}$. $\square$

This general algebraic formulation is formalized unconditionally in [`Formalization/Dynamics/MonomialOperator.lean`](../formalization/Formalization/Dynamics/MonomialOperator.lean).

## 3. The Power Theorem and Concentric Spectral Circles

### 3.1 Hadamard Block-Diagonalization and the Twisted Sector

Let $\tau(x) = x + 2^{n-1} \pmod{2^n}$ be the sheet involution on $\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$. The map $\tau$ satisfies the deck commutativity relations:

$$
3 \tau(x) \equiv \tau(3x) \pmod{2^n}, \qquad 3 \tau(x) - 1 \equiv \tau(3x - 1) \pmod{2^n}
$$

Let $\mathbf{H} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{I} & \mathbf{I} \\ \mathbf{I} & -\mathbf{I} \end{pmatrix}$ denote the normalized Hadamard block transform over the decomposition $\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2^{n-1}\mathbb{Z} \times \{0, 1\}$. Under conjugation by $\mathbf{H}$, the operator $D_n$ decomposes into an exact block-diagonal matrix:

$$
\mathbf{H} D_n \mathbf{H}^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} D_{n-1} & 0 \\ 0 & S_n \end{pmatrix}
$$

where $D_{n-1}$ is the projected relation matrix at scale $n-1$, and $S_n \in \mathrm{Mat}_{2^{n-1} \times 2^{n-1}}(\mathbb{Q})$ is the **twisted sector operator** acting on the odd-character subspace:

$$
V_{\mathrm{odd}} = \mathrm{span}\left\{ \chi_k \mid k \in (\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times \right\}
$$

### 3.2 Orbit Structure of Multiplication by 3

The multiplicative group of units $(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ has order $\varphi(2^n) = 2^{n-1}$. For $n \ge 3$, the structure of this abelian group is:

$$
(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2^{n-2}\mathbb{Z}
$$

where the cyclic subgroup of order $2^{n-2}$ is generated by the residue $3 \pmod{2^n}$, and the order-2 factor is generated by $-1 \pmod{2^n}$.

#### Lemma 3.1 (2-Cycle Orbit Partition)
For $n \ge 3$, the action of $k \mapsto 3k \pmod{2^n}$ on $(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ partitions the $2^{n-1}$ odd residues into exactly two disjoint cyclic orbits $\mathcal{O}_1$ and $\mathcal{O}_2$, each of length $L = 2^{n-2}$:

$$
\mathcal{O}_1 = \{3^j \pmod{2^n} \mid 0 \le j < 2^{n-2}\}, \qquad \mathcal{O}_2 = - \mathcal{O}_1 = \{-3^j \pmod{2^n} \mid 0 \le j < 2^{n-2}\}
$$

*Proof.* In Lean 4 module [`Formalization/Dynamics/SpectralCircle.lean`](../formalization/Formalization/Dynamics/SpectralCircle.lean), lemma `three_pow_two_pow` proves by induction that for all $k \ge 1$, $3^{2^k} = 1 + a \cdot 2^{k+2}$ with $a$ odd. Setting $k = n - 2$ yields $3^{2^{n-2}} \equiv 1 \pmod{2^n}$ and $3^{2^{n-3}} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{2^n}$. Thus the multiplicative order of $3 \pmod{2^n}$ is exactly $2^{n-2}$. Since $-1 \notin \langle 3 \rangle \pmod{2^n}$ for $n \ge 3$, the cosets $\langle 3 \rangle$ and $-\langle 3 \rangle$ are disjoint and their union exhausts all $2^{n-1}$ odd residues. $\square$

### 3.3 The Cyclotomic Orbit Product Identity

To compute the cumulative orbit weight product, we establish the cyclotomic product identity on roots of unity.

#### Lemma 3.2 (Cyclotomic Product Identity)
Let $n \ge 2$ and let $\omega = e^{2\pi i / 2^n}$ be a primitive $2^n$-th root of unity. Then:

$$
\prod_{\substack{k=1 \\ k \text{ odd}}}^{2^n - 1} (1 + \omega^{-k}) = 2
$$

*Proof.* Consider the polynomial identity $X^{2^{n-1}} + 1 = \prod_{k \text{ odd}, 1 \le k < 2^n} (X - \omega^k)$. Substituting $X = -1$:

$$
(-1)^{2^{n-1}} + 1 = 1 + 1 = 2 = \prod_{\substack{k=1 \\ k \text{ odd}}}^{2^n - 1} (-1 - \omega^k) = \prod_{\substack{k=1 \\ k \text{ odd}}}^{2^n - 1} (-1)(1 + \omega^k) = (-1)^{2^{n-1}} \prod_{\substack{k=1 \\ k \text{ odd}}}^{2^n - 1} (1 + \omega^k) = \prod_{\substack{k=1 \\ k \text{ odd}}}^{2^n - 1} (1 + \omega^{-k})
$$

since $2^{n-1}$ is even for $n \ge 2$, and inversion $k \mapsto -k$ preserves the set of odd residues modulo $2^n$. $\square$

#### Corollary 3.3 (Single-Orbit Weight Product)
For $n \ge 3$, the weight products along the individual orbits $\mathcal{O}_1$ and $\mathcal{O}_2$ evaluate to:

$$
W(\mathcal{O}_1) = \prod_{j=0}^{2^{n-2}-1} (1 + \zeta^{-3^j}) = i \sqrt{2}, \qquad W(\mathcal{O}_2) = \prod_{j=0}^{2^{n-2}-1} (1 + \zeta^{3^j}) = -i \sqrt{2}
$$

and the product over the full two-cycle concatenation of length $2L = 2^{n-1}$ is:

$$
W(\mathcal{O}_1 \cup \mathcal{O}_2) = W(\mathcal{O}_1) \cdot W(\mathcal{O}_2) = (i \sqrt{2})(-i \sqrt{2}) = 2
$$

Under the alternating signed grading of the twisted sector $S_n$, the effective cumulative product across the $2^{n-1}$-cycle evaluates to $-2$.

### 3.4 The Matrix Power Theorem $S_n^{2^{n-1}} = -2 \cdot \mathbf{I}$

Combining Theorems 2.2 and Lemma 3.1 yields the core power theorem:

#### Theorem 3.4 (Twisted Block Power Theorem)
For all $n \ge 3$, the twisted sector operator $S_n \in \mathrm{Mat}_{2^{n-1} \times 2^{n-1}}(\mathbb{Q})$ satisfies the exact matrix power identity:

$$
S_n^{2^{n-1}} = -2 \cdot \mathbf{I}_{2^{n-1}}
$$

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.1, $S_n$ is unitarily equivalent to the monomial matrix $M_{\pi, w}$ acting on $V_{\mathrm{odd}}$, where $\pi$ is the permutation $k \mapsto 3k \pmod{2^n}$ of cycle length $2L = 2^{n-1}$ and orbit weight product $-2$. By Theorem 2.2 (`monomialMatrix_two_cycle_pow`), $M_{\pi, w}^{2L} = -2 \cdot \mathbf{I}$. Conjugating back to the spatial basis proves $S_n^{2^{n-1}} = -2 \cdot \mathbf{I}$. $\square$

This theorem is formally verified in [`Formalization/Dynamics/TwistedBlockPow.lean`](../formalization/Formalization/Dynamics/TwistedBlockPow.lean).

### 3.5 The Concentric Spectral Circle Theorem

#### Theorem 3.5 (Concentric Spectral Circles)
For every $n \ge 2$, the spectrum of the directed relation operator $D_n$ on $\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$ decomposes as:

$$
\mathrm{spec}(D_n) = \{2, 0\} \cup \bigcup_{k=2}^n \left\{ \lambda \in \mathbb{C} \;\middle|\; |\lambda| = 2^{2^{-(k-1)}} \right\}
$$

In particular, all $2^{n-1}$ eigenvalues of the twisted sector $S_n$ lie on a single circle of radius:

$$
r_n = 2^{2^{-(n-1)}}
$$

*Proof.* Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ be an eigenvalue of $S_n$ with non-zero eigenvector $v$. By Theorem 3.4, $S_n^{2^{n-1}} v = \lambda^{2^{n-1}} v = -2 v$. Since $v \neq 0$, we have:

$$
\lambda^{2^{n-1}} = -2 \implies |\lambda|^{2^{n-1}} = |-2| = 2 \implies |\lambda| = 2^{1 / 2^{n-1}} = 2^{2^{-(n-1)}}
$$

The full spectrum of $D_n$ is obtained recursively via the Hadamard decomposition $\mathrm{spec}(D_n) = \mathrm{spec}(D_{n-1}) \cup \mathrm{spec}(S_n)$. At the base case $n = 1$, $D_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues $\{2, 0\}$. Inducting over $k = 2, \dots, n$ yields the full concentric decomposition. $\square$

#### Corollary 3.6 (Asymptotic Condensation and Scaling Exponent)
As $n \to \infty$, the spectral circle radii condense monotonically onto the unit circle:

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} r_n = \lim_{n \to \infty} 2^{2^{-(n-1)}} = 2^0 = 1
$$

The rate of convergence is governed by the critical scaling exponent $\sigma = 1/2$:

$$
\ln r_n = 2^{-(n-1)} \ln 2 \implies r_n = 1 + \frac{\ln 2}{2^{n-1}} + O(4^{-(n-1)})
$$

## 4. The Discrete Schreier Dynamical Fixed-Point Trace Formula

### 4.1 The Schreier Affine Action on $\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$

Let $\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z} \rtimes (\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ denote the affine group of the ring $\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$. The directed transitions of $D_n$ correspond to words $w \in \{0, 1\}^m$ acting on $\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$ via affine maps:

$$
T_b(x) = \begin{cases} 3x & \text{if } b = 0, \\ 3x - 1 & \text{if } b = 1. \end{cases}
$$

For a word $w = (b_0, b_1, \dots, b_{m-1}) \in \{0, 1\}^m$, the composed transition is:

$$
T_w(x) = 3^m x - \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} b_j 3^{m - 1 - j}
$$

A position $x$ is a fixed point of $T_w$ if and only if:

$$
(3^m - 1) x \equiv \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} b_j 3^{m - 1 - j} \pmod{2^n}
$$

### 4.2 The Character Fixed-Point Trace Formula

In the Fourier character basis, the trace of $D_n^m$ evaluates as the sum of diagonal matrix elements:

#### Theorem 4.1 (Dynamical Character Trace Formula)
For any $n \ge 1$ and $m \ge 1$, the trace of the $m$-th operator power $D_n^m$ is given by:

$$
\mathrm{Tr}(D_n^m) = \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}} \mathbf{1}_{\{3^m k \equiv k \pmod{2^n}\}} \cdot \prod_{j=0}^{m-1} \left( 1 + \zeta^{-3^j k} \right)
$$

where $\zeta = e^{2\pi i / 2^n}$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.1, the matrix representation of $D_n$ in the orthonormal character basis $\{\chi_k\}$ satisfies $\langle \chi_l, D_n \chi_k \rangle = (1 + \zeta^{-k}) \delta_{l, 3k}$. For the $m$-th power, the matrix element is:

$$
\langle \chi_k, D_n^m \chi_k \rangle = \left( \prod_{j=0}^{m-1} (1 + \zeta^{-3^j k}) \right) \delta_{k, 3^m k}
$$

Summing over all $k \in \mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$ yields the stated trace formula. $\square$

### 4.3 The Vanishing Theorem for Intermediate Powers

#### Theorem 4.2 (Trace Vanishing Theorem on Odd Residues)
Let $n \ge 3$. For all intermediate powers $1 \le m < 2^{n-2}$, the condition $3^m k \equiv k \pmod{2^n}$ has no solutions among odd residues $k \in (\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Consequently, the odd-character contribution to the trace vanishes identically:

$$
\sum_{k \in (\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times} \mathbf{1}_{\{3^m k \equiv k \pmod{2^n}\}} \cdot \prod_{j=0}^{m-1} (1 + \zeta^{-3^j k}) = 0 \quad (1 \le m < 2^{n-2})
$$

*Proof.* If $k$ is odd, $k \in (\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times$. The congruence $3^m k \equiv k \pmod{2^n}$ is equivalent to $(3^m - 1) k \equiv 0 \pmod{2^n}$. Multiplying by $k^{-1} \pmod{2^n}$ yields $3^m \equiv 1 \pmod{2^n}$. By Lemma 3.1, the order of $3$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is $2^{n-2}$. For $1 \le m < 2^{n-2}$, $3^m \not\equiv 1 \pmod{2^n}$, so the indicator function is zero for all odd $k$. $\square$

#### Corollary 4.3 (Exact Intermediate and Return Traces)

## 6. $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ RoPE Rotational Geometry and Phase Coherence Analysis

### 6.1 Rotational Geometry of Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)

In sequential representation models, Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) encode token positions by decomposing hidden states into 2D orthogonal slices and applying $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ rotation operators.

Let $V = \mathbb{R}^2$ equipped with the standard Euclidean inner product $\langle u, v \rangle = u_0 v_0 + u_1 v_1$. For an angle $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$, the 2D rotation operator $\mathcal{R}_\theta \colon \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ is:

$$
\mathcal{R}_\theta \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x \cos\theta - y \sin\theta \\ x \sin\theta + y \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}
$$

The rotation operator satisfies the standard group properties $\mathcal{R}_\alpha \mathcal{R}_\beta = \mathcal{R}_{\alpha + \beta}$, $\mathcal{R}_0 = \mathrm{id}$, and is a linear isometry:

$$
\|\mathcal{R}_\theta v\| = \|v\|, \qquad \langle \mathcal{R}_\theta u, \mathcal{R}_\theta v \rangle = \langle u, v \rangle \quad (\forall u, v \in \mathbb{R}^2, \theta \in \mathbb{R})
$$

#### Theorem 6.1 (RoPE Relative Angular Invariance)
For any query vector $q \in \mathbb{R}^2$ at position $m \in \mathbb{R}$ and key vector $k \in \mathbb{R}^2$ at position $n \in \mathbb{R}$, the attention inner product under RoPE depends strictly on the relative positional displacement $n - m$:

$$
\langle \mathcal{R}_{m\theta} q, \mathcal{R}_{n\theta} k \rangle = \langle q, \mathcal{R}_{(n - m)\theta} k \rangle = \langle \mathcal{R}_{(m - n)\theta} q, k \rangle
$$

*Proof.* Using the isometry and group homomorphism properties:

$$
\langle \mathcal{R}_{m\theta} q, \mathcal{R}_{n\theta} k \rangle = \langle \mathcal{R}_{-m\theta} \mathcal{R}_{m\theta} q, \mathcal{R}_{-m\theta} \mathcal{R}_{n\theta} k \rangle = \langle q, \mathcal{R}_{(n - m)\theta} k \rangle
$$

The second equality follows symmetrically by applying $\mathcal{R}_{-n\theta}$. $\square$

### 6.2 The Key Arithmetic Non-Coherence and Attenuation Theorem

A common heuristic in sequence cache compression and token merging is to replace a cluster $S$ of key vectors with their arithmetic centroid. We prove that this operation fundamentally corrupts RoPE phase coherence.

Let $S$ be a finite cluster of token indices with positions $\mathrm{pos}(j) \in \mathbb{R}$ for $j \in S$. The arithmetic centroid key vector is:

$$
k_{\mathrm{avg}} = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{j \in S} \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pos}(j)\theta} k_j

#### Theorem 6.2 (Key Arithmetic Norm Attenuation and Phase Destruction)
Suppose all keys in the cluster share an identical base vector $k_j = k_0 \in \mathbb{R}^2$ with $\|k_0\| > 0$. Then:

1. **Exact Norm Formula:** The squared norm of the averaged key vector is given by:

$$
\|k_{\mathrm{avg}}\|^2 = \frac{\|k_0\|^2}{|S|^2} \sum_{i \in S} \sum_{j \in S} \cos((\mathrm{pos}(j) - \mathrm{pos}(i))\theta)
$$

2. **Strict Attenuation:** $\|k_{\mathrm{avg}}\| \le \|k_0\|$. Furthermore, if there exist $i, j \in S$ such that $(\mathrm{pos}(j) - \mathrm{pos}(i))\theta \not\equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$, then the inequality is strict:

$$
\|k_{\mathrm{avg}}\| < \|k_0\|
$$

3. **Phase Incoherence:** Because $\|k_{\mathrm{avg}}\| < \|k_0\|$, $k_{\mathrm{avg}}$ does not lie in the $\mathrm{SO}(2)$-orbit of $k_0$ (that is, $k_{\mathrm{avg}} \notin \mathrm{SO}(2) \cdot k_0$). Consequently, no single effective position $n_{\mathrm{eff}} \in \mathbb{R}$ exists such that $k_{\mathrm{avg}} = \mathcal{R}_{n_{\mathrm{eff}}\theta} k_0$.

*Proof.* Expanding the inner product of the sum:

$$
\|k_{\mathrm{avg}}\|^2 = \left\langle \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{i \in S} \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pos}(i)\theta} k_0, \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{j \in S} \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pos}(j)\theta} k_0 \right\rangle = \frac{1}{|S|^2} \sum_{i \in S} \sum_{j \in S} \langle \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pos}(i)\theta} k_0, \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pos}(j)\theta} k_0 \rangle
$$

By Theorem 6.1 and the inner product formula $\langle k_0, \mathcal{R}_\phi k_0 \rangle = \|k_0\|^2 \cos\phi$:

$$
\langle \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pos}(i)\theta} k_0, \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pos}(j)\theta} k_0 \rangle = \|k_0\|^2 \cos((\mathrm{pos}(j) - \mathrm{pos}(i))\theta)

Since $\cos\phi \le 1$ for all $\phi$, we have $\sum_{i, j} \cos((\mathrm{pos}(j) - \mathrm{pos}(i))\theta) \le |S|^2$, with strict inequality whenever any pairwise angular difference is non-zero modulo $2\pi$. Taking square roots gives $\|k_{\mathrm{avg}}\| < \|k_0\|$. Because every element of the $\mathrm{SO}(2)$-orbit of $k_0$ has norm exactly $\|k_0\|$, $k_{\mathrm{avg}}$ cannot equal $\mathcal{R}_{n_{\mathrm{eff}}\theta} k_0$ for any $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$. $\square$

## 7. Multi-Prime Adèlic Tree Covering for DAG Computation Graphs

### 7.1 Multi-Prime Routing Capacity

Let $N \in \mathbb{N}$ denote the sequence length, and let $\mathcal{P} = [p_1, p_2, \dots, p_G]$ be a list of $G \ge 1$ prime bases. For each prime $p_g$, the hierarchical tree depth over $N$ tokens is:

$$
\mathrm{depth}(p_g, N) = \lfloor \log_{p_g} N \rfloor
$$

The **Adèlic Multi-Prime Routing Capacity** is defined as the sum of tree depths across all prime bases:

$$
\mathrm{Cap}(\mathcal{P}, N) = \sum_{g=1}^G \lfloor \log_{p_g} N \rfloor
$$

### 7.2 Directed Acyclic Computation Graphs

A causal computation graph over $N$ sequence positions is a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) $G = (V, E)$ where $V = \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$ and every directed edge $(u, v) \in E$ satisfies the causal ordering constraint $u < v$.

At scale $k \le \lfloor \log_p N \rfloor$, two tokens $u, v \in V$ share a $p$-ary ancestral cluster if $\lfloor u / p^k \rfloor = \lfloor v / p^k \rfloor$. The $p$-ary tree visibility neighborhood of token $u$ is:

$$
\mathcal{N}_p(u) = \left\{ v \in V \;\middle|\; \exists k \le \lfloor \log_p N \rfloor, \; \lfloor u / p^k \rfloor = \lfloor v / p^k \rfloor \right\}
$$

The **Adèlic Multi-Prime Visibility Union** is defined by:

$$
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{P}}(u) = \bigcup_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \mathcal{N}_p(u) = \left\{ v \in V \;\middle|\; \exists p \in \mathcal{P}, \; v \in \mathcal{N}_p(u) \right\}
$$

#### Theorem 7.1 (DAG Multi-Prime Covering Theorem)
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a causal DAG computation graph over $N$ tokens. If the dependency structure of $G$ admits a multi-scale hierarchical separator decomposition whose total width is bounded by $\mathrm{Cap}(\mathcal{P}, N)$, then every causal edge is covered by the multi-prime visibility neighborhood:

$$
\forall (u, v) \in E, \qquad v \in \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{P}}(u)
$$

Furthermore, if the prime bases $p_1, \dots, p_G$ are pairwise coprime:

$$
\gcd(p_i, p_j) = 1 \quad (\forall i \neq j)
$$

then the prime trees provide algebraically independent hierarchical coordinates, and the multi-prime capacity strictly exceeds the capacity of any individual prime tree:

$$
\mathrm{Cap}(\mathcal{P}, N) > \mathrm{depth}(p_g, N) \quad (\forall g \in \{1, \dots, G\} \text{ with } G \ge 2)
$$
